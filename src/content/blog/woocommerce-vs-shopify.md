WooCommerce and Shopify are the two platforms we are asked about most for online stores. Both are excellent. The right choice depends on how much control you want, who will run the store, and how you prefer to pay for it.

## The short version

- **Choose Shopify** if you want to start selling quickly and would rather not deal with hosting, security or updates.
- **Choose WooCommerce** if you want full control, already use WordPress, or need a store that works exactly the way your business does.

## What each one is

**Shopify** is a hosted platform. You pay a monthly subscription, and Shopify runs the servers, security, updates and checkout. You customise your store with themes and apps.

**WooCommerce** is a free plugin that turns a WordPress website into a store. You choose your hosting, and you own and control everything, which also means someone has to maintain it.

## Cost: how you pay is different

Neither is simply "cheaper". The money goes to different places.

- **Shopify**: a monthly plan, plus paid apps for extra features, plus payment processing fees. Shopify also charges a transaction fee when you use a payment provider other than its own Shopify Payments.
- **WooCommerce**: no platform fee, but you pay for hosting, any premium extensions or theme, payment provider fees, and maintenance.

For a small store, the totals are often similar. As a store grows, app subscriptions on Shopify and hosting and extension costs on WooCommerce both scale, so compare them for your own product range and order volume.

## Ease of use

Shopify is built for store owners. Adding products, managing orders and setting up shipping is straightforward, and there is nothing to update.

WooCommerce lives inside WordPress. If your team already edits a WordPress site, it will feel familiar, and your blog, pages and store sit in one dashboard.

## Control and flexibility

This is WooCommerce's biggest advantage. You can change almost anything: checkout fields, product types, pricing rules and integrations. Your data lives on your own hosting.

Shopify is flexible through themes and its app store, but some parts, especially checkout, are more restricted, and you work within Shopify's rules.

## Payments

Both support major payment providers such as Stripe and PayPal, plus cards and wallet payments. Check which providers support your country and currency before choosing, as availability varies.

## SEO and content

Both platforms can rank well. WooCommerce benefits from WordPress's content tools: blogs, landing pages and SEO plugins such as Yoast or Rank Math. Shopify handles the essentials well, with some limits on URL structure.

If content marketing is a big part of your plan, WooCommerce's WordPress foundation is a strong advantage.

## Maintenance and security

With Shopify, security and updates are handled for you. With WooCommerce, WordPress, the theme and plugins need regular updates, backups and monitoring, either by your team or a [maintenance plan](/website-maintenance).

## Which one we recommend

We ask four questions:

1. **Does your team already use WordPress?** Yes: WooCommerce keeps everything in one place.
2. **Do you want zero technical upkeep?** Yes: Shopify.
3. **Do you need unusual product or checkout logic?** Yes: WooCommerce.
4. **How important is content alongside the store?** Very: WooCommerce, usually.
