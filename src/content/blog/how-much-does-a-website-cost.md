"How much does a website cost?" is the first question almost every client asks us, and the honest answer is: it depends on what the website needs to do. A five-page site for a local electrician and an online store with 500 products are both "websites", but they are very different projects.

This guide explains what actually drives the price, so you can compare quotes properly and budget with confidence.

## The type of website sets the starting point

The biggest factor is what kind of site you need. Roughly from simplest to most involved:

- **Brochure or landing page.** A few pages that explain who you are and how to contact you. Quick to build, but still needs good design and copy to work.
- **Business website.** Service pages, an about page, a blog and contact forms, usually on a content management system such as WordPress so your team can edit it. This is what most small and medium businesses need.
- **Online store.** Products, cart, checkout, payments, shipping and tax rules. More pages, more testing and more integrations. See our [e-commerce development](/ecommerce-development) service.
- **Web application or platform.** User accounts, dashboards, bookings or listings, like a rental platform. These are priced on features rather than pages.

## What moves the price within each type

### Number of pages and how different they are

Ten service pages built from one layout cost far less than ten pages that each need their own design. Tell your agency how many pages you need and which ones are unique.

### Template or custom design

A customized theme is faster and cheaper. A custom design built around your brand and customers takes longer but makes you stand out, which matters in competitive markets. Many projects sit in between: a custom homepage and key pages, with simpler templates for the rest. Our [website design](/website-design) process covers both.

### Features and integrations

Booking systems, multi-language content, CRM or email-marketing connections, member areas, calculators and custom search all add development and testing time. List the features you truly need at launch, and which can wait.

### Content: who writes it and who takes the photos

Content is the most underestimated cost. If you supply finished text and images, the project moves faster. If your agency writes the copy, sources images or writes content for SEO, that is extra work, and usually worth it, because content is what persuades visitors and what Google ranks.

### SEO from the start

A site can be built "SEO-friendly" (clean structure, fast pages, meta tags) or it can launch with keyword research, optimized service pages, structured data and a submitted sitemap. The second costs more but saves you rebuilding pages later. Read more about our [SEO optimization](/seo-optimization) work.

## Costs after launch

The build is a one-off; running a website is ongoing. Budget for:

1. **Domain name**, renewed every year.
2. **Hosting**, monthly or yearly, depending on traffic and performance needs.
3. **Licenses** for premium plugins, themes or apps.
4. **Maintenance**: software updates, backups, security monitoring and small changes. See our [website maintenance checklist](/blog/website-maintenance-checklist) for what this involves.

## Freelancer, agency or website builder?

- **DIY website builders** are the cheapest way to get online, but you trade away flexibility, speed and often SEO control.
- **Freelancers** can be excellent value for a clearly defined project. Check they can cover design, development and SEO, or who will.
- **Agencies** cost more than a single freelancer but give you a team: design, development, SEO and support, with continuity if someone is away.

## How to get an accurate quote

The more precise your brief, the more precise the quote. Before you ask, prepare:

- What the website must achieve: calls, bookings, sales or applications.
- A list of pages and must-have features.
- Two or three websites you like, and why.
- Who will provide the text and photos.
- Your deadline and a budget range. A range lets an agency tell you what is realistic, instead of guessing.

## Signs a quote is too cheap

A very low price usually means something is missing. Ask whether the quote includes: mobile testing, SEO setup, content entry, SSL, backups, and who owns the website and domain afterwards. If ownership stays with the agency, you are renting, not buying.

## The bottom line

A website is priced by what it has to do, how custom it is and how much of the content and SEO work is included. The cheapest quote is rarely the cheapest website once rebuilds and lost inquiries are counted.
