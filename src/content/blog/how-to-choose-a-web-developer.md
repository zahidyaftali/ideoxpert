Choosing the right web developer is one of the most important decisions for a growing business. Your website is often the first place customers meet you, and a bad build costs you twice: once to pay for it, and again to fix or replace it.

The problem is that there are thousands of website developers to choose from. Some are solo freelancers, some are large agencies, and many are somewhere in between. Prices range from a few hundred dollars to tens of thousands for what sounds like the same thing.

This guide explains what a web developer actually does, how a freelance web developer compares with a website development company, what a website should cost, and the questions to ask before you sign anything. By the end, you will know what kind of developer fits your project and budget.

## What does a web developer actually do?

A web developer builds and maintains the technical side of your website: the code, the pages, the forms, the store and everything that makes the site work. The job titles overlap, so it helps to know the difference before you hire.

- **Web designer:** plans how the site looks and feels, including layout, colors, fonts and user experience. Usually works in tools like Figma.
- **Front-end developer:** turns that design into a working website that looks right on phones, tablets and desktops.
- **Back-end developer:** builds what you don't see, such as databases, user accounts, payments, booking systems and integrations.
- **WordPress website designer:** specializes in building sites on WordPress, often with a custom theme so you can edit content yourself.
- **Full-stack developer:** handles both front-end and back-end work.

For most small and medium businesses, you don't need to hire each role separately. You need one person or team who can cover design, development, speed and basic SEO, and who will still be around after launch.

### When a freelance web developer makes sense

A good freelancer is a smart choice for a simple brochure site, a landing page, or small fixes to an existing site. You talk directly to the person doing the work, and the price is usually lower.

The risk is that everything depends on one person. If they get busy, sick or move on, your project or your support can disappear overnight. Freelancers also rarely cover design, development and SEO equally well.

### When a website development company makes sense

A web development company is the better choice when your site needs to bring in leads or sales, when it has features like booking, payments or a store, or when you want someone to look after it for years.

You get a team: a designer, developers, and someone responsible for project management and quality. Work continues even if one person is away, and there is usually a clear process, a written scope and a support plan.

### What about "website developers near me"?

Many people search for website developers near me because they assume they need someone local. In practice, almost all web projects today run remotely, even when the agency is in the same city. Calls happen on Zoom, files are shared online, and progress is reviewed on a staging link.

What matters more than distance is clear communication, overlapping working hours, a written contract and a portfolio you can verify. A remote web development company can often give you agency-level quality at a lower price than a local one.

## How much do website developers cost?

There is no single price for a website, because "a website" can mean a five-page business site or a full online store with hundreds of products. The cost depends mostly on these factors:

| Cost factor | Lower cost | Higher cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number of pages | 1 to 5 pages | 10+ pages or many templates |
| Design | Adapted from a ready-made layout | Fully custom design |
| Content | You provide the text and images | Copywriting and photography included |
| Features | Contact form | Booking, payments, member areas, integrations |
| Online store | No store | Store with many products and payment options |
| SEO | Basic setup | Keyword research and ongoing content |
| Support | Launch only | Monthly maintenance plan |

Where your developer is based also affects the price. Agencies in New York, London or Toronto charge local rates because their own costs are high. A remote team can often deliver the same quality for less.

## 10 questions to ask before you hire a web developer

Whether you're talking to a freelancer or a web development company, these questions quickly show who knows what they're doing.

1. **Can I see live websites you've built?** Ask for real links, not just screenshots. Open them on your phone and check how fast they load.
2. **Who will actually work on my project?** Some agencies sell the project, then pass it to someone you never meet.
3. **What exactly is included in the price?** Get a written scope: pages, features, revisions, content and launch tasks.
4. **How many revisions do I get?** Unlimited revisions often means unclear scope. A clear number is a good sign.
5. **Who owns the website and the code?** You should own your domain, your hosting account and your website files once you've paid.
6. **Will I be able to update the site myself?** Ask to see the admin area or CMS before you agree.
7. **Is basic SEO included?** At minimum: page titles, meta descriptions, headings, image alt text, a sitemap, fast loading and mobile-friendly design.
8. **What happens after launch?** Ask about bug fixes, updates, backups and the monthly cost of support.
9. **How will we communicate?** Agree on the channel, the response time and the overlap in working hours.
10. **How are payments structured?** Payments in stages protect both sides: for example half to start and half at launch, or three milestones on a larger project.

### Red flags to watch for

- No portfolio, or only templates you can find online
- A price far below everyone else's, with no clear explanation
- No written contract or scope
- They keep your domain or hosting in their own name
- Promises like "guaranteed #1 on Google"
- Slow or vague replies before you've even paid

## Do you need a WordPress website designer?

WordPress powers a large share of the web, and for good reason. It's flexible, widely supported, and lets you edit your own pages and blog posts without calling a developer.

A WordPress website designer is a good fit if:

- You want to publish blog posts or update pages yourself
- Your site is mainly for information and leads (services, contact forms, bookings)
- You want a large choice of developers who can support the site in future

WordPress is not always the best choice. A large online store may run better on Shopify, which we compare in [WooCommerce vs Shopify](/blog/woocommerce-vs-shopify). A custom web app or customer portal may need a framework like Laravel or React. A good web development company will recommend the right platform for your goals, not the one they happen to prefer.

One tip: ask for a custom or lightweight theme rather than a heavy page builder with dozens of plugins. Fewer plugins usually means a faster, more secure site that's easier to maintain. We cover that trade-off in [WordPress vs a custom-coded website](/blog/wordpress-vs-custom-website).
